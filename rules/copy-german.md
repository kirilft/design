# Copy: German

Apply these rules to visible German copy, including headings, labels, and buttons. Use German as used in Germany (de-DE).

- Use German quotation marks („Speichern“) and single German quotation marks for nested quotes (‚Speichern‘).
- Use the curly apostrophe (’) in forms such as „Wie geht’s?“ and for the possessive of a name ending in an s sound when it stands without an article (Max’ Profil). Write ordinary possessives and plurals without an apostrophe (Annas Profil, die Fotos). Preserve the spelling of official names.
- Use an en dash with spaces for a break in a sentence (Schnell – auch unterwegs). Use an en dash without spaces for compact ranges (2024–2026), and a hyphen for compounds such as E-Mail.
- Leave no spaces around slashes in short labels and values (Hell/Dunkel, 24/7).
- Use a decimal comma (19,90) and a nonbreaking space before units, currency symbols, and percent signs (5 km, 19,90 €, 20 %). Use 24-hour time and a nonbreaking space before „Uhr“ (09:30 Uhr, 09:30–17:00 Uhr).
- Use one space between sentences. Leave no space before a comma, period, colon, or question mark.
